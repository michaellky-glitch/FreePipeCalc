Tutorial — Hydronic — Thermal

How to use the THERMAL tab. Top ribbon reference, then sign, fluid, conditions, insulation, and results.

Hydronic discipline · THERMAL tab · v0.18.2

THERMAL sets temperature. It controls ambient, source water temperature, insulation, surface film, and the plausibility band. Thermal solves after hydraulics and uses the solved flow.

NOTE:

Click THERMAL in the top bar. The drawing hides. The thermal panel appears. Top ribbon is hidden here — mock-ups below show it for reference.

IMPORTANT:

Thermal does not change flow. It reads flow from the hydraulic solve. If you change a pipe size, the thermal result changes because flow changes — not because heat calculation feeds back.

1. Overview

THERMAL has five blocks:

  1. Sign — what +Q and −Q mean.
  2. Fluid — read-only echo from HYDRAULIC (Cp, density).
  3. Conditions — ambient and source water temperature.
  4. Insulation — thickness, k, surface coefficient, per-size table, loss per metre.
  5. Last solve — temperature range, duties, energy balance.

4. Sign convention

Sign — mock

−Q removes heat from the fluid. +Q adds heat to the fluid. A CHW coil is + (fluid gains heat? No — read the switch).

Panel shows heating = red, cooling = blue. Switch position tells you direction.

  • −Q — Heat leaves the fluid (chiller removes heat, hot pipe loses to ambient).
  • +Q — Heat enters the fluid (boiler adds heat, CHW coil adds heat to fluid).
  • The switch on the equipment panel shows heating (red) vs cooling (blue). Red is not a fault here — it is the direction.

5. Fluid (read-only)

Fluid — mock

Fluid: Water — as set on HYDRAULIC.

Cp = 4187 J/kg·K | Density = 998 kg/m³ | Quoted at 20 °C.

Set fluid on HYDRAULIC. THERMAL shows Cp for Q = ṁ·Cp·ΔT.

  • Cp is live in thermal. A wrong Cp scales every kW linearly.
  • Unverified fluids (e.g., glycol presets) show a warning. Check before you issue.

6. Conditions

Conditions — mock fields

Ambient air temperature (°C) — what the pipe loses to when insulated or bare.

Source Water Temperature (°C) — default leaving temperature. A source without its own temperature holds this.

  • Ambient — Air around the pipe. Sets the target for pipe loss. Leave at 20 °C for indoor plant unless you have measured data.
  • Source Water Temperature — Flow temperature reference (default 6 °C, chilled water). A sealed loop with no source finds its own level against ambient; if it cannot, this value is pinned at the highest duty outlet.

7. Insulation

Insulation — mock

Thickness (mm) — global default (50 mm default). Per-size table below. Per-pipe override on the pipe panel (including 0 = bare).

Thermal conductivity (W/m·K) — default 0.02 (polyurethane).

Outside surface coefficient (W/m²·K) — default 8 (still indoor air). h = 0 → adiabatic (no loss).

U′ = 1 / [ ln(ro/ri)/(2πk) + 1/(2πroh) ]. Table shows loss per metre per K for each size.

  • Thickness — Three levels: global default, per-size table, per-pipe value. Per-pipe wins. Blank in table = use global.
  • k — Insulation conductivity. Lower k = less loss.
  • ho — Outside film. Default is not sourced data. On a bare pipe, ho is the whole resistance, so the result depends strongly on it.
  • Pipe outlet — Tout = Tamb + (Tin − Tamb) · exp(−U′·L / ṁ·Cp). Exponential, so temperature approaches ambient and stops. No overshoot.

8. Plausibility band

Plausibility band — mock

Minimum temperature (°C) — default −50. Maximum temperature (°C) — default 50.

Outside band = error, not a number. For LTHW at 80 °C flow, raise the maximum.

  • Band is a sanity check. If a load has no sink, the solve goes to a ridiculous temperature. The band turns that into an error so you see it.
  • Suits chilled water by default. For heating circuits, set maximum above your flow temperature.

9. Last solve

Last solve — mock readout

Temperature range: min … max °C

Equipment duty: X kW | Pipe gain/loss: Y kW | Energy balance: closes (or imbalance kW out)

Reference: ambient — system finds its own level (if sealed, no source). Pinned at outlet of highest duty equipment if needed.

  • Read this after you solve. Nothing to solve yet = no readout.
  • Energy balance closes at steady state. If it shows kW out, that much heat has no sink — check plant or loads.
  • Mixing at junctions is flow-weighted: Tmix = Σ(ṁ·T) / Σṁ.
SUGGESTION:

After you set insulation, go to PIPING NETWORK, click SHOW > TEMPERATURE, and solve. Colour shows where heat is lost. Check CALCULATION > Thermal section for per-pipe loss.
