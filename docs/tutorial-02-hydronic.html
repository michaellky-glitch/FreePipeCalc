Tutorial 02: A multi-storey hydronic system

Build a six-storey chilled-water system: a central plant with duty and standby chillers, a floor copied up two risers, and the pumps controlled from the pressure at the highest coil.

Open examples/Tutorial 02.json and draw on it as you go — the plant room, the risers and each AHU floor are already labelled. This tutorial follows on from Tutorial 01.

1. Before you start

A six-storey building. Level 0 is the mechanical floor; Levels 1 to 5 are typical, each with one AHU room. You will draw the plant, build one floor, copy it up the building, and then let the pumps follow the load instead of running flat out.

Tutorial 01 built one loop by hand and explains the drawing tools, the property panels, and the pump, chiller and coil settings. Those are not repeated here. Do it first if you have not.

The load

QuantityValueWhere it comes from
Heat load per floor200 kWOne AHU on each of Levels 1–5
Total connected load1000 kW5 × 200 kW
Expected peak load800 kW80% of connected — the floors do not all peak together
Chillers3 × 400 kWTwo duty, covering the 800 kW peak. One standby.

The standby chiller earns its place twice over: it covers a duty machine taken out for maintenance, and it covers a load above the expected peak. You will draw all three and switch the third off.

NOTE:

The example file is a layout to draw on, not a finished model. The plant, the pipe routes and the sizes are already labelled on the canvas; the pipework is what you add. Work with those labels in front of you rather than from this page alone.

2. Set the project parameters

Set these before you draw. Each one is quicker to set now than to correct on fifty pipes later.

Naming

Open SETTINGS ▸ Equipment naming. The scheme used here is [Discipline]-[Floor]-[Type][Number], so the first chiller on the mechanical floor comes out as MECH-L0-WCH01.

IMPORTANT:

The source water temperature sets the fluid properties for the whole model — density, viscosity and specific heat all follow it. State your design supply temperature on the source; leaving it at the default calculates a 6 °C circuit as though it ran at room temperature. It shows in the design flows before you reach the friction at all: 200 kW across 7.5 K is 6.35 L/s at 6 °C against 6.38 L/s at 20 °C. The Calculation Method page, section 5.1, carries the correlations and their sources.

3. Draw the plant room

Go back to HYDRONIC, in DESIGN mode, on Level 0. Trace the plant room header and the routes marked on the canvas: the 125 mm supply and return headers, the 100 mm branches at each chiller lineup, and the bypass.

Add a source at the expansion tank. In a closed system the source is the datum — it fixes the pressure everything else is measured from — and it states the water temperature the model is calculated at.

NOTE:

Most chilled-water headers run at high level, and you would normally model that as its own level — L0-HEADER, say. This tutorial keeps the plant on one level to stay out of its own way. Nothing about the method changes if you add the header level.

4. The first chiller lineup

Equipment is modelled on a pipe, not floating beside one. Connect the supply and the return first, then put the machines into that pipework.

  1. Draw the branch from the return header, and the branch back to the supply header.
  2. Place a pump on the return branch with the PUMP tool.
  3. Place a heat source/sink between the return and the supply with the HEAT SOURCE/SINK tool — this is the chiller.
IMPORTANT:

Equipment is directional. Connected backwards it stops the flow rather than passing it. Place the pump first and orient it the way the water goes; equipment placed downstream then takes the pump's direction automatically.

IMPORTANT:

A pipe must be at least 1 m long to take a piece of equipment. Draw the branch before you try to place a machine into it.

Select the chiller and set its design properties.

ControlSet to
SizingManual
Cooling capacity400 kW
LWT setpoint7.5 °C
Design ΔT7.5 K

Manual sizing, not Auto: this is a machine you have selected, so its nameplate is a fact rather than something for the program to work out. On Auto it would report the capacity you need instead of holding you to the one you have.

5. Copy the lineup for chillers 2 and 3

The other two lineups are identical. Copy the first rather than drawing it twice.

  1. Click the return branch pipe, then hold Shift and click the supply branch pipe. That selects the whole lineup — both pipes and the machines on them.
  2. Copy with Ctrl+C, or COPY on the Edit ribbon.
  3. Paste with Ctrl+V and place the copy at the Chiller 2 position. Repeat for Chiller 3.
NOTE:

While you are placing a copy the preview tells you whether it will connect: a filled circle means a tee will be generated automatically, an empty one means it will not.

IMPORTANT:

Check both ends of every paste. A lineup joined at one end only will not carry water, and the drawing looks the same either way. If a tee was not generated, drag the pasted pipe off the header and back onto it.

Now switch the third lineup off. Select MECH-L0-WCH03 and its pump and set Status to Offline, or click the power icon beside the machine. That is the standby.

6. Take the risers up

Check that the view direction for Level 0 is looking up, then add a RISER to the end of the supply pipe and to the end of the return pipe.

NOTE:

If you see “Riser column placed. There is no level in the View Direction, so click this floor’s pipework on another level to connect it”, the floor is looking the wrong way. Change the view direction on the level panel and place the riser again.

7. Draw Level 1 and its AHU

Go to Level 1 and draw the pipework across to the AHU room, following the routes marked on the canvas. Join the branches to the riser markers rather than to loose pipe ends. Flow arrows appear when you close the loop, which is the quickest check that the floor is connected.

Place a heat exchanger between the AHU supply and return, and set it.

ControlSet to
Pressure drop100 kPa
Cooling / HeatingHeating
Capacity200 kW
Design ΔT7.5 K
Temperature limit (max)26 °C
Control valveAuto

The design flow fills itself in — about 6.35 L/s with a 6 °C source. Capacity, flow and ΔT are three ways of saying the same thing, so stating two of them gives the third.

NOTE:

“Heating” on a chilled-water coil is correct, and it catches everyone once. The sign convention is written from the water’s point of view: a cooling coil takes heat out of the room and puts it into the water, so it adds heat and is positive. The chiller takes it out again and is negative.

IMPORTANT:

The integrated control valve sits inside the quoted pressure drop, not on top of it. If your coil schedule lists the coil and its valve separately, add the two together and enter the total. An integrated valve is offered because machines often come with one, and because it saves drawing a valve you would only ever set to match the coil.

8. Copy the floor upward

Levels 2 to 5 are the same floor again. Copy it rather than drawing it four more times.

  1. Select Level 1 on the level panel and click C to copy its layout.
  2. Leave the target on New floor above and press Copy.
  3. Repeat from each new floor until Level 5 exists.

Each copy carries the pipework, the labels and the detail lines, extends the riser columns so the new floor joins the stack, and re-tags the equipment for the floor it lands on — Level 2’s coil arrives as MECH-L2-AHU01 without you touching it.

SUGGESTION:

Step through the levels afterwards and check the riser markers appear on every floor. A floor that copied but did not connect is the one fault that will not show itself until you simulate.

9. Sync the floors together

Five identical floors are easier to study if they move together.

  1. Select the Level 1 AHU. Under Control, set Sync part load % with to the Level 5 AHU.
  2. Repeat for Levels 2, 3 and 4.

Changing MECH-L5-AHU01 now drives the whole building, so you can sweep the load from one panel instead of five.

10. Simulate at peak load

Run a DESIGN calculation first so the pumps have curves, then switch to SIMULATE.

NOTE:

Simulating at full load reports that a setpoint cannot be held, and that is the model telling the truth: the chillers were sized for the 800 kW peak while the connected load is 1000 kW. The plant is not short — the building never asks for all of it at once.

Set the part load to 80%, the expected peak, and simulate.

What you should see
  • MECH-L0-WCH01 and MECH-L0-WCH02 report as overloaded.

That is a real finding rather than a modelling error. The chillers were sized on the room loads alone, and the pipework gains heat on its way round the building. CALCULATION ▸ Thermal shows how much. It is the kind of margin that is easy to leave out of a hand calculation and expensive to leave out of a plant.

Drop the part load to 79% to carry on.

Now look at MECH-L5-AHU01
  • Its control valve is part closed, throttling away pressure it does not need.
  • The pump VFDs are still at 100%.

That is a constant-speed system: the pumps push as hard as they can and every coil valve throttles against them. Nothing has told the pumps what the building actually needs.

11. Control the pumps from a sensor

Measure the pressure where it is hardest to deliver — the top floor — and let the pumps follow it.

  1. Switch to CONTROL mode and go to Level 5.
  2. Place a sensor on the AHU supply pipe and set Measures to Differential pressure.
  3. Pick the AHU return pipe as its reference pipe. The sensor now reads across the coil.
  4. Set the Δp setpoint to 110 kPa — the coil’s 100 kPa rating plus about 10% for the pipework around it.
  5. With the sensor still selected, click ADD CONTROL and link it to the first pump.
  6. Select the second pump and set Sync with to the first.
NOTE:

Link one pump to the sensor and sync the rest to it. Two pumps chasing the same reading fight each other; one leads and the others follow its speed.

12. Simulate again

Return to SIMULATE.

What you should see
  • The pumps slow down together as the sensor holds the top-floor pressure.
  • The coil valves open up, with less pressure to throttle against.
  • The pressure across the top coil settles at the setpoint instead of running high.

The saving is the difference between those two states, and it grows as the building unloads. Sweep MECH-L5-AHU01 down through 60% and 40% and watch the pumps follow it.

SUGGESTION:

If you would rather not choose the setpoint yourself, switch the sensor’s Δp setpoint to Auto. The solve then searches for the lowest differential that still holds every coil, which is the reset a real building management system performs. It costs a full control loop per trial, so it is slower than a fixed figure. Set the comparator to MIN if you want a floor under it.
